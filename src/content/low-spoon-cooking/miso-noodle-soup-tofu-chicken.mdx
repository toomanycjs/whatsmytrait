Brothy, salty and warming, this is what I want on a bad POTS day when I need fluid and salt as much as I need food. It's one small pan and about 10 minutes, most of which is waiting for the noodles. Tofu or cooked chicken both work, so use whichever you have in the fridge.

The cupboard and freezer ingredients last for ages, so once you've bought them, each bowl only needs the tofu or chicken.

## Why it's low spoon

- **The kettle does most of the heating.**
- **No knife needed.** The spinach goes in as frozen blocks, the tofu can be torn into chunks with your fingers and cooked chicken slices tear easily too.
- **One small pan,** and most of the time is spent waiting.
- **Miso keeps for months** in the fridge, so it's a handy thing to have in.

## Method

<RecipeMethod steps={frontmatter.recipe.steps} />

**Microwave version (saves the pan):** put the noodles and spinach in a large microwaveable bowl, cover with boiling water and microwave for 3 to 4 minutes. Stir in the tofu or chicken and give it another minute, then stir in the miso. Use a big bowl, as it can bubble up.

## Storing leftovers

The soup itself is best eaten straight away, as the noodles go soft sitting in the broth. Leftover tofu keeps in a tub covered with water in the fridge; change the water daily and use it within the time on the packet. Opened cooked chicken should be used within the time the pack says, usually a couple of days.

## A note on salt

Miso is very salty: a tablespoon is roughly 2g of salt, before any soy sauce. If you've been advised to increase your salt intake for POTS, that's arguably the point of this recipe. If you need to limit salt, use a reduced-salt miso, use a little less of it and skip the soy sauce.

## Things to know

- **Allergens:** contains soy (miso, tofu and soy sauce), and the egg noodles contain wheat and egg. Rice noodles make it egg and wheat free, but some miso contains barley, so check the label if gluten matters for you.
- **Tofu:** silken tofu is softer and more traditional in miso soup, but it falls apart easily. Firm tofu is easier to handle and tears neatly.
- **Chicken:** use ready-cooked chicken only. Raw chicken won't cook safely in this time.
