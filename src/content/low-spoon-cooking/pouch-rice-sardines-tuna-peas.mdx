This is what I make when there's almost nothing left in the tank. It's barely cooking: a pouch of rice, a tin of fish and some frozen peas, all done in one bowl in the microwave. It's still a proper meal with plenty of protein, and everything in it keeps for months in the cupboard or freezer, so it's there on the days you can't get to the shops. Sardines in tomato sauce bring their own sauce; tuna wants a splash of soy sauce or a squeeze of mayo.

The pouch says it serves two, but as a whole meal I eat the lot.

## Why it's low spoon

- **No chopping, no hob, no oven.** Just the microwave.
- **Ring-pull tins,** so no tin opener. Check the tin before you buy, as most own-brand sardines and tuna have one.
- **One bowl and one fork** to wash.
- **No fresh ingredients,** so nothing goes off before you get round to it.

## Storing leftovers

This is best eaten straight away. Cooked rice needs a bit of care: if you don't finish it, cool it and get it into the fridge within an hour, eat it within a day and only reheat it once, until piping hot.

## A note on salt

Most of the salt comes from the tinned fish and the soy sauce. If you've been advised to increase your salt intake for POTS, a bit of extra soy sauce or a pinch of salt is an easy win. If you need to limit salt, choose fish in spring water, skip the soy sauce and use pepper, chilli or lemon instead.

## Things to know

- **Sustainability:** I've moved away from mackerel because stocks are under pressure. Sardines are generally a better choice. For tuna, look for MSC certified or pole and line caught on the label.
- **Sardine bones:** they're soft enough to eat without noticing and are a good source of calcium. Boneless sardines exist but cost more.
- **Tuna and pregnancy:** the NHS advises that if you're pregnant you have no more than 4 medium-sized tins of tuna a week, because of mercury.
- **Oily fish:** sardines count towards your oily fish for the week; tinned tuna doesn't.
- **Gluten:** standard soy sauce contains wheat. Tamari is usually gluten free, but check the label.
