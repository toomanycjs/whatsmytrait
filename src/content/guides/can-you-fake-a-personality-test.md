Yes. A self-report questionnaire records what you say about yourself, and people asked to answer as an ideal job applicant raise their scores on every Big Five scale by about half to one standard deviation. That is why these tests are weak tools for selecting staff and good tools for self-description: when you answer for yourself, there is nothing to gain by faking.

## The evidence

Viswesvaran and Ones (1999) meta-analysed studies in which people were instructed to fake good or fake bad on personality inventories. Instructed faking shifted every Big Five scale, by about half a standard deviation when groups answering honestly were compared with groups told to fake, and by more when the same people answered honestly and then again as instructed. Birkeland and others (2006) compared real job applicants with people answering for research. The shifts were smaller: applicants scored higher mainly on conscientiousness and emotional stability, by under half a standard deviation, and differed little on extraversion, openness and agreeableness. Real applicants shade their answers rather than rewrite them, mostly on the traits they expect the job to want.

## What faking does not mean

It does not mean scores are meaningless. Faking is a deliberate act with a motive, and the people you are compared with on this site answered online for themselves, with no one to impress. A result you dislike does not mean you faked it in the other direction; self-report also carries self-image, mood on the day and the standard you hold yourself to, and those are part of what is measured. The research does not say applicants are dishonest people: shading an answer towards the job is what almost everyone does when a job depends on it.

## What it means for your results

The test's own instruction is to describe yourself *as you generally are now, not as you wish to be in the future*, in relation to people of your own age and sex. If you follow it, your percentiles mean what they say. If you have taken a Big Five test as part of a job application, expect its conscientiousness and emotional stability scores to sit above the ones you get here, and treat the ones here as the better reading. If you are unsure whether you answered as you are or as you would like to be, take it again in a few weeks and compare the two attempts on the same strip.

The Big Five test takes about twelve minutes, is scored on your device, and compares you with 410,376 people who had no reason to fake.
